How to use these

Copy each one into your own documents, replace anything in square brackets, and keep them in the templates folder from Module 12.

They are deliberately short. A long form does not get returned and a long contract does not get read, so everything here is the shortest version that still does the job.

1. First call questions

Ask these in order. Fifteen to twenty-five minutes.

  1. Tell me what the business does, and how long you have been going.
  2. When somebody wants to order or book at nine at night, what happens right now?
  3. How do most new customers find you at the moment?
  4. What do people say about you when they leave?
  5. If you could fix one thing about how people reach you, what would it be?
  6. Most of what I do lands between [one and three thousand] to build, plus a monthly amount. Does that sound like the right neighborhood?

End with: "I am going to write this up and send you three options tomorrow morning. Have a look, tell me which one fits, and then we will talk about dates."

2. Intake form

Send with the deposit request. Keep it to these six.

  • Business name, spelled exactly as it should appear, including punctuation
  • Phone, address, and opening hours, including holiday closures
  • Menu or service list with prices, or a note saying it is coming
  • Any logo, photographs, or colors you already have
  • Three competitor sites you like, and one sentence on why for each
  • Who is the one person I should send everything to?

3. The quote

One page. Send it the day after the call.

[Business name] — website options[Date]

What I understood. [Their words, not yours. "You told me people call after nine and there is no way for them to order."]

Option one — Hand It Over. I build the site, connect it to a domain you own, hand over all the files, and you own everything. $[1,500], finished within [three weeks] of getting your menu.

Option two — Keep It Running. I build it and then look after it: menu updates, hours, photos, monitoring, and I track your domain renewal. $[800] to build, then $[600] a month.

Option three — Build It Out. Everything in option two, plus online ordering so customers can pay through the site. $[4,000] to build, then $[900] a month. Takes around [six weeks].

What happens next. Tell me which option fits and I will send an invoice for the deposit. Work starts when that clears and your menu arrives.

4. The scope paragraph

Send before any building starts. Get a written yes back.

To confirm what we agreed: a [one-page] site for [business name] with a [menu section, about section, and contact details], on a domain you own. Includes one round of changes after you review it. Price is $[1,500], half now and half at launch, finished within [three weeks] of getting your [menu].

Anything outside that — online ordering, booking, extra pages — I am happy to do, and I will quote it separately.

5. The change order

Three parts. Send it, then wait.

You asked for [online ordering through the delivery apps]. That is $[900] and about [two weeks], and it would push the launch to the [ninth]. Reply yes and I will start [Monday].

6. The invoice

Eight items, no more.

  • Your business name and contact details
  • Client business name
  • Invoice number
  • Date issued
  • Plain description of the work
  • Amount due
  • How to pay
  • Due on receipt

7. The care plan agreement

The document that stops a monthly fee being cancelled in March.

[Business name] care plan — $[600] per month

Included every month:

  • Up to [four] small changes: hours, prices, photos, staff, specials
  • Monthly check that the site loads, forms arrive, and nothing has broken
  • Domain renewal tracked, and you are told before it lands
  • A saved backup copy of the site
  • Response within [one business day], by phone or email
  • A short quarterly note: orders taken, calls from your Google listing, anything that broke

Not included, quoted separately: new pages, new features, anything connecting to another company's system, photography, and repairing anything I did not build.

Monthly, cancel any time with [30] days notice. The domain and all files remain yours.

8. The seven-point draft check

Before anybody outside sees the site.

  1. Business name spelled correctly everywhere, including the browser tab
  2. Menu or service list appears above the story about the business
  3. Every invented price clearly labeled as a placeholder
  4. No invented phone number, address, or hours remaining
  5. Main image looks like a photograph, not a drawing
  6. Page loads properly on a real phone, which you checked
  7. Every button does something when tapped

9. The twenty-point pre-launch check

The full list lives in Module 11. Print that table and keep it beside you.

Short version of the five that catch the most: real phone test, no sideways scrolling, real phone number dialed, name and address matching the Google listing character for character, and a stranger opening it cold.

10. The launch message

Send on launch day, with the final invoice.

Your site is live at [rosastamales.com]. I checked it on a phone and a laptop, the phone number dials, and the address opens a map to the right place. The domain is registered in your name and renews on [March 14th]. Here is the folder with your logo, colors and files. [Invoice for the balance attached.]

13. The referral ask

Thirty days after launch, once something good has happened.

Glad the orders are coming through. Who else around here do you think has the same problem you had? If you can think of two, I will go and see them.

14. The six rehearsed answers

Full versions in Module 22. The short forms:

"Can't I do this myself with AI?" — "Yes, honestly. The building was never the hard part. It is the domain, the ordering, the listing, and remembering to update it in six months."

"Why so much?" — "If one extra order a night is worth thirty dollars, this pays for itself in two months."

"Cheaper?" — "I can do less for less. What I would rather not do is charge less and quietly do a worse job."

"How do I know you won't disappear?" — "The domain goes in your name, not mine. If I vanish tomorrow you still own everything."

"The last person did it free." — "Free stops getting updated. I charge monthly so it stays somebody's job."

"Let me think about it." — "Of course. Is it the price, the timing, or somebody else you want to run it past?"

The four to make firstIf you only build four of these today: the intake form, the quote, the scope paragraph, and the invoice.

Those four cover the whole distance from a first conversation to being paid, and everything else can wait until you have a real client.
