Sourced market data appendix

Every number used in this book, where it came from, and which ones are shakier than they look.

ReferenceFog index 8.086 min read

Why this exists

Two reasons, and the second matters more.

The first is that you may need to quote a figure to a client, and a number you cannot source is a number you should not say out loud.

The second is that all of this expires. Prices, commissions and platform rules change constantly, so this page exists to tell you when to check again rather than to be trusted forever.

All figures were gathered in August 2026. Check anything before quoting it to a paying client.

The 2026 market

AI cut the cost of a simple website by sixty to eighty-five percent. Complex builds held their price or rose. Sites that cost around five thousand dollars three years ago now build for under fifteen hundred. (GoodFirms website development cost survey, 300+ companies across 31 countries, fielded April–May 2026.)

Sixty percent of agencies charge one thousand to three thousand dollars for a basic site or first version, and seventy-one percent deliver in two to four weeks. (Same survey.)

AI-built sites: 33.5% quote five hundred to fifteen hundred dollars, and 23% quote under five hundred.(Same survey.)

Monthly retainers: 30% of agencies charge five hundred to fifteen hundred dollars a month, 28% charge fifteen hundred to three thousand. Yearly maintenance commonly runs fifteen to twenty percent of the original build cost. (Same survey.)

Ninety-eight percent of web development agencies now use AI, along with 93% of designers and 82% of developers. (Same survey.)

The recurring revenue finding

This is the spine of the entire book, so it deserves the clearest sourcing.

Projects under five thousand dollars are profitable about a third of the time. Projects above five thousand dollars are profitable more than half the time.(The Admin Bar, State of the WordPress Agency 2026, 622 respondents across 51 countries, fielded February–March 2026.)

Agencies with no recurring revenue are unprofitable 58.3% of the time. Once a quarter of income is recurring, that figure drops below ten percent. (Same survey.)

Fewer than one in five solo designers earn most of their income from recurring work, across every revenue band. Two out of three gross under fifty thousand dollars a year. Median rate $92.75 an hour. (Web Designer Academy, State of Web Designer Pricing 2025, 208 designers, 60% US.)

Only fifteen percent of solo designers charge for discovery, and those who do are twice as likely to land five-thousand-dollar-plus projects. (Same survey.)

Who is buying

Eighty-three percent of US small businesses have a website, up from 64% in 2018. Of the 17% without one, 34% say it is not relevant to their industry and only 16% cite cost. (Clutch, State of Small Business Websites 2025, 406 US small business owners, fielded August 2025.)

Who builds them: 45% agency, 37% in-house, 9% the owner personally, 9% freelancer, 1% friend or family.(Same survey.)

Ninety percent plan to invest in their site in the next twelve months. Top priorities: 58% redesign, 53% speed, 45% mobile, 37% search, 32% payments. (Same survey.)

⚠️ Conflicting figures. Other sources say one in three small businesses have no web presence at all, or that only 40% maintain a dedicated website. These use different definitions and cannot be reconciled. Present it as a range: somewhere between 17% and 33% have no website.

Local search and the listing gap

Only about 35% of small businesses have a properly established Google Business Profile.(BrightLocal local SEO statistics.)

Eighty-four percent of consumers searched for a local business in the past three months. Seventy-five percent decide in under thirty minutes, and 28% in under five. Roughly three-quarters of local searches happen on a phone.(BrightLocal, 2026.)

Accessibility and legal risk

More than five thousand digital accessibility lawsuits were filed in 2025. Sixty-four percent of defendants had under twenty-five million dollars in revenue. Around 70% involved e-commerce and roughly 21% food service. Businesses using accessibility overlay widgets were sued at essentially the same rate.(UsableNet, ADA web lawsuit trends, January 2026.)

Ordering, booking and payments

Checked August 2026. These change often — verify before quoting.

Delivery marketplace commissions. DoorDash 15% Basic, 25% Plus, 30% Premier for delivery, 6% pickup. Uber Eats 20% Lite, 25% Plus, 30% Premium, pickup 7–10%. Grubhub 5%/15%/20% marketing commission plus delivery starting at 10%. (Each platform's own merchant pricing page.)

Own-website ordering. DoorDash Online Ordering is commission-free with processing around 2.9%–3.3% plus thirty cents. Uber Eats Webshop charges 2.5% plus twenty-nine cents. Grubhub Direct has no commission but does not publish its processing rate. (Vendor pages.)

⚠️ Two vendors contradict themselves. DoorDash states 2.9% + $0.30 on one page and 3.3% + $0.30 for the same package on another. Uber Direct is "from $7.99" on one page and "as low as $6.99" on another. Quote a range, never a single number.

Payments. Stripe 2.9% + $0.30 for domestic online cards, no monthly fee. PayPal checkout 3.49% + fixed fee, card processing 2.99%. Square 3.3% + 30¢ online on the free plan, 2.9% + 30¢ on paid plans. (Vendor pricing pages, August 2026; PayPal's page updated 15 July 2026.)

Booking. Setmore free up to 200 appointments a month including a website widget. Calendly free for one event type, around $10 a seat to remove branding. Square Appointments free with unlimited staff calendars, $49 and $149 monthly tiers. Acuity from about $20 a month, no free plan. (Vendor pricing pages.)

Integration timelines, all vendor self-reported. DoorDash online ordering live in under 24 hours. Grubhub Direct "in minutes." Uber Eats API integration: four to eight weeks, requiring an NDA and licensing agreement. DoorDash Marketplace API: certification required, 14-day partner onboarding SLA, sub-1% order failure rate. Toast full POS: four to six weeks.

The historical eras

2006. 42% of US adults had home broadband. Internet Explorer held 82–86% of browsers. Dreamweaver 8 cost $399. No US government job code for "web developer" existed until 2010. Around 35% of small firms had a website, based on a 2002 baseline. (Pew Research; The Register; CreativePro; BLS; SBA.)

2016. US web design services was a $24.4B industry in 2015. WordPress ran 27% of all websites. Wix had 97.36 million registered users and 2.465 million paying. Ninety-five percent of freelancers charged under $5,000 a site and only 1% cleared $10,000. Seventy percent sold maintenance plans, at $25–$100 a month. About half of US small businesses had no website. (IBISWorld; W3Techs; Wix FY2016 results; Elegant Themes Divi survey, 5,000+ respondents, June 2016; Clutch, February 2016.)

2026. US web design services roughly $47B, with industry employment falling about 4.8% a year. (IBISWorld, via secondary citation.)

What could not be verified at all

  • Any independent study of implementation time for restaurant ordering integrations
  • Any published rate card for integration work specifically
  • Any causal study of AI's effect on web design freelancer income
  • Non-employer share of the web design trade specifically
  • 2006 US web design industry revenue
  • NYC and other regulated-market delivery fee caps

How to use this page with a clientQuote ranges, not single numbers. "Somewhere between fifteen and thirty percent" survives being checked. "Twenty-five percent" does not.

Open the vendor page while they watch. It takes thirty seconds and it makes you the person who checks rather than the person who guesses.

Refresh this page every six months. Set a reminder. Most of the platform figures will have moved.
